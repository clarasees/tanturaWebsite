To the Sea: Sumoud in Tantura — Palestinian coastal heritage and practices of joy

To the Sea is a People and Ruins project resurfacing coastal life of Palestinians, focusing on the erased coastal village al-Tantura, located 24KM south of Haifa.

In Arabic, al-Tantura means the ‘peak’, referring to the small hill surrounding the seashore on which the Palestinian village once stood. Post-1948 occupation, only a shrine, a fortress, an ancient wall, and a few of the houses remain, and Tantura is completely erased on popular maps and is tagged as Tel Dor, an Israeli recreational area with swimming facilities.

Tantura Map → reconstructs the displaced Palestinian coastal heritage of al-Tantura through present-day satellite mapping, interspersed with knowledge drawn from oral histories of Palestinian fisherman and community organizer Sami Ali, based in Jisr al-Zarqa.

In 1948, Tantura was the site of a massacre carried out by Israel’s Haganah (Alexandroni Brigade). The victims were buried in mass graves — one of them now beneath a parking lot for the nearby Tel Dor beach. Learn more about the 2023 Forensic Architecture research → Watch the documentary →

Palestinians by the sea continue to practice resilience even in the most ordinary acts of living.

Tantura Life (page link) is a compendium of coastal heritage, a record preserved for future generations to reference and a testament of joy and resilience of persistent Palestinian living by the sea. It brings together present and past traditions, livelihoods, sports and forms of recreation alongside oral histories and research into the textures of everyday life in al-Tantura: its geography, its people, its rhythms along the water.
